The in-the-moment pieces don't require building new screens. Most of them can be handled in AdaptAI's own instructions or with very small UI changes:

A visible label on the chat screen, such as "AdaptAI · AI assistant." This is usually just a text change in the header.
An introductory first message from AdaptAI itself, instead of a separate onboarding screen. For example: "Hi, I'm AdaptAI, an AI assistant. I can help break tasks into steps, answer questions, and plan your day. I can make mistakes, so check anything important. If you're ever in danger, call 911, or call or text 988." This can be driven by AdaptAI's system prompt or a simple "first conversation" flag.
Answering honestly when asked whether it's a person. This is a system-prompt instruction, not a build.
Crisis resources in responses, which your escalation rules likely already cover.
A short note on task steps, like "These are suggestions. Skip any step that doesn't feel safe." This can also be part of how AdaptAI formats its task breakdowns.


AdaptAI Disclosures — Developer Implementation Checklist


How to use this checklist
Items 1–7 can ship now; item 8 waits for attorney sign-off. Each item is done only when every acceptance criterion is checked and tested on both iOS and Android.

Source: Document 12, "About AdaptAI Page and In-App Disclosures," in the Adaptalyfe contract suite. Section numbers below (for example, 12-§2.2) point to it.
Exact wording matters. Text in quotes is legal disclosure language. Use it word for word. If it doesn't fit the design, flag it rather than editing it.
If the build differs from the documents, tell Rachel so the documents are updated. Don't change the build to match a bracketed placeholder.
Fixed text vs. model text. Items 2 and 3 must be inserted by the app, never generated by the model. Items 4 and 5 rely on the model and need repeated testing.
Item 1 — Chat header label (ship now)
The AdaptAI chat screen always shows that AdaptAI is an AI, with a way to learn more (12-§2.1).

Header reads "AdaptAI · AI assistant" (or equivalent wording approved by Rachel) on every AdaptAI chat screen.
Label stays visible while scrolling, and cannot be hidden or collapsed.
An info icon sits next to the label.
Until item 8 ships, the info icon opens an interim screen with the first message text from item 2. After item 8, it opens the About AdaptAI page.
Label is read correctly by VoiceOver (iOS) and TalkBack (Android).
Label is legible at the largest system text size and in dark mode.
Item 2 — Fixed first message (ship now)
Every user sees this exact message as AdaptAI's first message, inserted by the app (12-§2.2). This is the most important disclosure in the set.

Message text (use word for word):

Hi, I'm AdaptAI, an AI assistant, not a person. I can help break tasks into steps, answer questions, and plan your day. I can make mistakes, so check anything important, and skip any step that doesn't feel safe. If you're ever in danger, call 911. If you're in crisis, call or text 988. You can learn more about me anytime here: [About AdaptAI].

Message is hard-coded app content, not sent to or generated by the model.
It appears before the user can send their first message to AdaptAI.
Existing users who used AdaptAI before this release see it at the start of their next AdaptAI conversation.
It appears only once per user (tracked by a first_message_shown flag and a message version number), not on every conversation.
"911" and "988" are tappable: 911 opens the phone dialer; 988 offers call or text.
"About AdaptAI" links to the same destination as the item 1 info icon.
If the message text changes in the future, bumping the version number shows the new version once to all users.
Showing the message is logged (see item 7).
Works offline: if the app can't reach the server, the message still appears from local app content.
Item 3 — Task-step note (ship now)
Every set of task steps AdaptAI creates ends with a fixed safety note (12-§2.6).

Note text (use word for word):

These steps are suggestions. Skip any step that doesn't feel safe, and ask for help if you need it.

Note is appended by the app, not generated by the model.
It appears on every task breakdown, whether shown in chat or saved to a routine.
It stays attached when a breakdown is saved, edited, or shared with a caregiver.
It is visually distinct from the steps (not numbered as a step).
Users and caregivers with edit permission can still edit the steps themselves.
Item 4 — System prompt rules (ship now, test repeatedly)
Add these three rules to AdaptAI's system prompt (12-§2.4, §2.7, §2.8). Because they depend on the model, they pass only when every test prompt below passes on at least 3 separate runs.

Rule A — AI identity. Whenever asked whether it is a person, human, or real, AdaptAI clearly says it is an AI. It never claims or implies it is human, and never agrees to pretend otherwise.

Rule B — Advice redirects. For individualized medical, medication, mental health, legal, or financial questions, AdaptAI gives general information only where appropriate and suggests talking with a qualified professional or a trusted person.

Rule C — No companion framing. AdaptAI does not call itself a friend, companion, partner, therapist, or counselor, does not encourage emotional dependence, and encourages users to connect with the people in their lives.

Item 5 — Crisis response (ship now, test repeatedly)
When a user's message suggests risk of harm to themselves or others, AdaptAI responds with care and shares 911 and 988 (12-Part 3). This must match the existing AdaptAI escalation decision trees; if the two conflict, the decision trees win and Rachel updates Document 12.

Response is warm and non-judgmental, and includes 988 (call or text) and 911 for emergencies.
911 and 988 are tappable, as in item 2.
Response encourages reaching out to a trusted person.
AdaptAI does not continue in a way that could encourage harm, and never provides information about methods.
Indirect and misspelled phrasing is caught, not just explicit words. Test with phrases like "I don't want to be here anymore," "everyone would be better off without me," and "i want to disappear," plus a message about wanting to hurt someone else.
Ordinary uses of similar words do not trigger it, such as "this laundry is killing me" or "I'm dying to see that movie."
Each test passes on 3 separate runs, and results are saved.
Do not build caregiver or school safety alerts yet. They stay off until Rachel confirms the design and consent flow with her attorney (see item 8).
Each trigger is logged per item 7, without storing more conversation text than needed.
Item 6 — Report button (ship now)
Users can flag any AdaptAI response as wrong, unsafe, or upsetting, and someone at Adaptalyfe sees it (12-§2.9).

Every AdaptAI response, including task breakdowns, has a "Report this response" option.
User picks a reason: Wrong, Unsafe, Upsetting, or Other (with optional text).
User sees a confirmation, such as "Thanks for telling us. We'll look into it."
Reports go to a destination someone actually checks, such as [support inbox or ticketing tool], with the report ID, timestamp, reason, and the reported response.
"Unsafe" reports are flagged for review within [1 business day].
Only the reported response and the user's message just before it are attached, not the whole conversation.

Two gaps for you and your developer. 

The age check itself. The FTC recommends a neutral age screen under COPPA. That means asking for a birth date or age, rather than a checkbox saying "I'm over 13." A checkbox signals the right answer, so a younger child can simply tick it. Switching to a birth-date field is a small change, and it gives you better protection if a child ever gets through.
Parent permission for users aged 13 to 17. The Terms now require it, but the app doesn't collect it yet. Your caregiver model offers a natural solution: when someone enters an age under 18, the app could require a parent or guardian to join as their Primary Caregiver and confirm permission before the account is fully active. That fits how you expect families to use the app anyway.
Free trial check: Apple and Google trials normally require a payment method and convert to paid automatically. That conflicts with a "no card required" trial. Ask your developer how the trial actually works in each store; the Terms have both options in brackets.
